NAMING,
ROUND TWO.

My first list leaned on plain words that other apps already own. This round starts from the product’s emotion and goes much wider, then cuts hard.

WHAT LOGOSYSTEM TAUGHT ME

  • What I could and couldn’t see. The site’s App Icons collection lists 149 icons by name and category, including close neighbours such as Turf, Any Distance, Gentler Streak, GO Club and PUSH. The page text doesn’t carry the artwork, so I used its structure and what I know of how well-known icons are built, and copied nothing.
  • Icons are forms, not diagrams. The strong ones are one bold shape on a confident ground, with a single twist: a gap, a cut, an overlap. My last logo was a UI widget, a slider, which is why it felt cheap.
  • Names are short and soft to say. Amie, Yume, Doz, Bump, Comet, Thru, Turf. They fit a mouth and an icon label.
  • The category is not empty. Screen Freedom pairs you with a friend, and Lock On uses pledges. “Ahead”, my earlier idea, appears in that collection as an established 4.7-rated fitness app, so it stays out.

THE BRIEF

What the name carries: the tension of one more scroll, the pride of holding out, a friend watching the scoreboard. How it should sound: one or two syllables, hard consonants for rivalry, easy to say in “duel me on ___”. What it must avoid: betting words, existing brands, anything you can’t spell after hearing it.

About 60 names in all. Five survived: they mean something, sound strong, and have no obvious collision in this category.

THE FIVE

MY RECOMMENDATION

Onside. Football’s line is the one line you must stay behind to keep playing, which is your limit. “Stay onside” says it, and your lost screen can say “Offside”, with a flag. Football is the world’s sport and a Nigerian passion without being local, so the name is global and neutral. The logo is one idea you can own: a marker that almost touches the line.

The honest risks: a youth-sports parent app called Onsides exists on iOS, a different category but a near neighbour, so check the Play Store and trademark carefully. The stress falls on the second syllable, so it’s a little softer than a name like Parry. Second choice: Holdout, whose “H” is the strongest letterform here. Third: Parry, the most distinctive sound.
