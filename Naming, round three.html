NAMING,
ROUND THREE.

Round two failed on taste: Volo read generic, Onside needed a paragraph before it meant anything. Round three went back to the schoolyard and cut to one rule: the name is a command — an instruction you can shout across the street. Nopeek commanded the most, so it was the pick. This page shows the decision and the two who nearly came with it.

THE WINNER

NOPEEK

The icon. An eye outside its lid, poking past the bar. The bar exists to hold it — the eye snuck round anyway. That cheating angle is the whole product, in two shapes, and it looks right at every size down to a notification dot.

  • Meaning. The rule of the whole game, said in six letters: don’t peek. You open to know; your friend already knows.
  • Say it aloud. “Duel me on Nopeek.” Two syllables landing on hard P and K — call of a referee, not a startup deck.
  • Lost state. “He peeked.” The scoreboard says it, your friend says it, you say it about yourself. The confession is the fun part.
  • Grammar. Always one word, always one poke of forbidden capital-N. Wordmark carries the rule so nobody writes it apart.
  • Trademark field check. Nopeek-style compounds are mostly self-descriptive and clear of existing apps (peek is used in a screen-tracking app only as a word, not a name). Still run a proper Play Store and IP-screen before launch.
  • LogoSystem fit. One bold shape, one confident ground, one twist: a gap the eye already slipped through.

The honest risks: a compound of two plain words (no Peek) can take over, so the one-word lock is your job forever; and “peek” will never be world-class in formality — if the product later tilts to corporate wellness, the name is Pollywog at a board meeting.

THE RUNNERS-UP

Facedown

Meaning. The gesture everyone can see: phone on its face until the duel ends. Flip to cheat and you’re literally facedown in defeat too.

Say it aloud. “Duel me on Facedown.”

Why second. Better sentence, worse name — cool low consonants and a slab-of-glass icon that never resolves into a single bold shape.

Gotcha

Meaning. The moment of capture: cave to the scroll and your friend’s scoreboard booms “Gotcha!” It is the victory shout itself.

Say it aloud. “Duel me on Gotcha.”

Why third. Warmer, funnier, but every app in the world can say “gotcha” in a sentence — the name owns no monopoly and the icon (a hand on a wrist) has been drawn a thousand times.

Their lessons stand: soft-sounding beats clever, meaning-on-first-hearing wins. Nopeek is the first name on any round that met both, plus one — it sounds like a foul in a schoolyard, not a deck slide.

CALLING NEXT STEPS

  • Clear the mark. Run the name through App Store and Play Store across this category and register the domain Nopeek.app in the same batch.
  • LogoSystem round three. Carry this eye-past-the-bar shape into the tile collection, and test at 24 px before falling for it.
  • Streak scope check. Settling a name means the slogan should also land in a friend’s mouth: “Nopeek tonight?” is your first ticker candidate.
